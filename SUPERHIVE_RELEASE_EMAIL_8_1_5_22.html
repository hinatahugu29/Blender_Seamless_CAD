Seamless CAD

Version 8.1.5.22 is out

Sketches can finally see the solid underneath them — plus a way to cut a part with a plane.

Until now a sketch was an island. You could put its plane on a face, but lining a new profile up with a corner of the part you were sketching on was done by eye. Two things change that.

The cursor snaps to the part you are sketching on

Geometry Snap pulls the sketch cursor to the corners of the solids already in your Feature Tree. An orange square marks the one it has caught.

The corners are projected onto the sketch plane first, so a corner that sits below or above the plane can still be snapped to — at the point straight through it. It is on by default, and it takes precedence over grid snap: being dragged off a corner you just met and onto the nearest grid step is not helpful.

Project an existing edge into the sketch

The new Project Geometry tool brings geometry you have already modelled into the sketch. Click an edge for that one edge, or hold Shift and click a face to bring in its whole outline. There is a toggle to bring it in as construction lines instead, so it guides the drawing without becoming part of the profile.

A circle lying parallel to the sketch plane arrives as a real circle with its radius intact, not as a ring of short line segments. What you get is the shape seen looking along the sketch plane's normal, the way a shadow falls — so a slanted face arrives foreshortened. If you wanted that face at its true size, make it the sketch plane with on Face instead.

One limit worth knowing before you rely on it: what you project is a snapshot. Change the solid it came from and the projected lines stay where they were. They do not break, and they do not follow. The panel repeats this while the tool is active. Projecting again gets you a current copy.

Cut a part with a plane

Split (Plane) sits beside Cleanup under Topology. The plane is placed the way a Mirror plane is — choose the axis it faces, then drag the feature in the viewport to position the cut. Keep decides what survives: the side the normal points at, the other side, or both halves as separate solids. Keeping both divides the part without removing anything. A plane that misses the shape entirely leaves it alone rather than emptying it.

Two smaller things

The Feature Tree stops burying itself. A curve that came from a sketch can carry thirty or more points, and selecting it used to spill every one of them down the panel. They are behind a disclosure triangle now, closed by default, with the count still visible while folded.

Sketch on Face works when nothing is selected. The button used to require an active object, which it never actually needed — clicking empty space in the viewport was enough to grey it out.
Updating
Download the build for your platform from your Superhive library and install it through Blender's Install from Disk, the same way as before.

Quit every Blender window first. This release changes the geometry program as well as the add-on, and on Linux and macOS that program cannot be replaced while it is running. The add-on will tell you in the console if it ends up talking to the old one, but starting clean avoids it entirely.
Three things to be straight about

A large Inset on a face that already has a fillet can still come out wrong. This is a defect rather than a design limit. It is written up on the manual's Limitations page, and a report with the model is genuinely welcome.

macOS remains the least proven platform. The build comes from the same automated pipeline as the others and is checked to compile, bundle, start, answer and package completely — but no individual feature has been confirmed inside Blender on a Mac by the developer. If you are on a Mac, your report is the only way that changes.

The Linux package does not currently pass the full test suite. It was run against the shipped Linux build before this release and it does not complete: it reaches the undo check and Blender aborts there, about six runs in seven. Older builds of the add-on behave exactly the same, so this is not something in the new work — and nobody has reported it while using Blender normally, which may be because the test drives undo from a headless session rather than by pressing Ctrl+Z. It is open and being looked at. The suite is 62 checks and passes on Windows on every change.
